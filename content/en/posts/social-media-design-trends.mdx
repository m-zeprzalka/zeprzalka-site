Welcome to our graphic design workshop! 🎉 Today we move on to applying specific effects to our graphics.

The goal is to **design one professional-looking social media post** — a 1080×1080 px or 1080×1350 px Instagram square, for example. We will work in **Figma** and **Photopea**. I will show you how to achieve particular modern effects, and your job is to **adapt them live** to your own design.

Rather than making "something", we will work from concrete examples.

## Exercise: effects and styles to use in your work

During the session I will show you how to reach each of the effects below quickly. We will practise them in the order given, and you will try to weave them into your own design.

### 1. High contrast / neubrutalism

Strong saturated colours (often pure black plus one neon or primary colour), simple shapes, and hard, unblurred shadows.

- **How? (Figma):** pure colours (#000000, #FFFFFF, #FF0000), simple layouts, shadows with 0% blur.

### 2. Masking

Creative use of images and typography — text "filled" with a photo, or a photo cut out into an unusual shape.

- **How? (Figma/Photopea):** use a shape or text as a mask for the image layer. In Figma the function is "Use as Mask".

### 3. Minimalism

The art of saying a lot with very little. What matters here is whitespace, precise typography and a deliberate visual hierarchy.

- **How? (Figma):** plenty of empty space, one strong colour accent, clean typography.

### 4. Aurora / blur gradient

Very soft, flowing colour transitions reminiscent of the northern lights. Ideal for creating depth and organic backgrounds.

- **How? (Figma):** several coloured ellipses plus a "Layer Blur" effect at a very high value (150–250).

### 5. Noise / texture (grain)

Adds analogue character and depth. It breaks up perfectly smooth digital surfaces.

- **How? (Photopea/Figma):** apply the "Noise" filter in Photopea, or lay a ready-made noise texture over the design in Figma with a blend mode of "Overlay" or "Soft Light".

### 6. Glassmorphism

Elements that look like semi-transparent frosted glass sitting on a colourful background.

- **How? (Figma):** a shape with reduced fill opacity (20–40%) plus a "Background Blur" effect.

### 7. Bold fonts

Treating text as the main graphic element rather than an addition. The letters become the image.

- **How? (Figma):** use large, expressive typefaces (serif or sans) as the centrepiece of the composition, often with very tight leading or a creative arrangement.

### 8. Pattern

Using repeating elements (icons, shapes) to build an interesting background or frame.

- **How? (Figma):** create a simple component (an icon, say) and repeat it on a regular grid, or use a plugin for generating patterns.

## How will we work? (our workflow)

1.  **Choosing a brief (10 min):** decide which subject you will take on and find one or two photos or assets to use.
2.  **Setup (5 min):** prepare a file in Figma (an artboard of 1080×1080 px).
3.  **Working through the effects together (about 75 min):** this is the main part. I will demonstrate each effect live, starting with high contrast. Your job is to follow my steps while adapting the techniques to _your own_ design — your colours, fonts and photos.
4.  **Q&A and presentation (the remaining time):** a short review of a few pieces, questions and feedback.

## Summary

Today's class is not an exam — it is a playground. The point is to get your hands dirty in Figma and Photopea, test some new techniques, and see that modern, complicated-looking effects are often within easy reach.
